Saru

Privacy policy

Saru – Japanese OCR Dictionary, for Android. Effective 8 November 2026.

In short

Saru keeps your photos, the text it reads, the words you look up and your settings on your phone. It has no account, no ads and no tracking. The one exception is Google ML Kit, the text recognition built into the app, which sends Google technical diagnostics about how it runs. It never sends your photos or text.

What stays on your phone

  • Photos. You choose a photo, or take one with your camera app. Saru reads the text in it on your phone. A photo taken from Saru is kept in the app's private storage until the next one replaces it. Photos are never uploaded.
  • Text and lookups. The text Saru reads, what you type, and the dictionary results stay in the app. The dictionary is built into the app and works offline.
  • Settings. Your deck and note type choices and your theme are stored in the app.
  • Flashcards. When you tap +, Saru adds a card to AnkiDroid, another app on your phone, with your permission. What AnkiDroid does with your cards, such as syncing them, is covered by AnkiDroid's own policy.

What Google ML Kit sends to Google

Saru reads text with Google ML Kit, which runs on your phone. To diagnose problems and measure how it is used, ML Kit sends Google technical information: your device's manufacturer, model and Android version, available machine learning hardware, the app's name and version, identifiers that are reset when the app is reinstalled and aren't meant to identify you or your device, performance measurements such as how long recognition took, image size, and error codes. It is sent encrypted (HTTPS), and Google doesn't pass it on to third parties. It never includes your photos, the recognized text or the words you look up. This can't be turned off in Saru.

See Google's ML Kit data disclosure and privacy policy.

Permissions

  • Internet: the app's screens are served by a small server inside the app, reached on your phone only (127.0.0.1), and ML Kit uses the connection for the diagnostics above. Saru itself sends nothing over the internet.
  • Network state: added by Google ML Kit, so it sends its diagnostics only when you're online.
  • AnkiDroid database: to add the cards you ask for. Android asks you once.

Saru doesn't ask for camera, storage, location or contacts permissions. Your camera app takes photos, and Android's photo picker shares only the photo you choose.

Children

Saru is a dictionary for learners aged 13 and over and isn't directed at children under 13.
